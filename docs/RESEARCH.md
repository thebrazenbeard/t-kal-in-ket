# Research: what T’Kal-in-ket is and what this engine adds

## Fictional source

*Star Trek: Discovery*, season 3, episode 7, “Unification III” (2020), stages Michael Burnham's petition before a three-peer Ni'Var quorum, assisted by her mother as advocate. The procedure is associated with logic, science, Surak and the search for deep truths. It has withdrawal and peer dismissal pathways. See [Memory Alpha](https://memory-alpha.fandom.com/wiki/T%27Kal-in-ket) and [TrekCore's contemporary review](https://blog.trekcore.com/2020/11/star-trek-discovery-review-unification-iii/).

The dramatic ceremony includes adversarial credibility attacks. In empirical inquiry this requires qualification: credibility is relevant when the reliability of a source/method matters, not because a participant is unpopular or imperfect. An evidence-centered process must explicitly reject irrelevant ad hominem arguments.

## Grounded design choices (our extensions)

The three invented lenses (Logic, Pragmatism, Bridge) do not correspond to three fixed fictional peer offices. A separate independent machine/host cannot be inferred from a name. Peer plurality is useful only insofar as it yields different evidence, methods, error modes or falsifiers. A shared model with three prompts is a panel of perspectives, not an independent quorum.

We borrow from scientific preregistration, thesis defense and adversarial review. In cases involving behavioral learning, report held-out predictions and results, baseline and controls, leakage checks, experimental costs, negative transfer, and reproducibility. Neither agreement nor a passing code test demonstrates real-world improvement.
